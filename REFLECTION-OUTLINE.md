# Reflection paper outline (4–7 pages, due Sat Dec 12, 2026)

The syllabus asks the reflection to cover four things: why you chose the medium, how you integrated SLA research, what population the project serves, and how it could be used as a teaching tool outside the class. The outline below maps each to the finished site. Target roughly 1,200–1,800 words.

## 1. Opening (½ page)
- Hook: the hero line — most families already have a language policy; the question is whether it is the one they want.
- State the concept you taught: Family Language Policy (FLP) as beliefs, practices, and management (Spolsky, 2012; King, Fogle & Logan-Terry, 2008).
- Thesis of the project in one sentence: raising a child with more than one language is not about one perfect rule but about an intentional, flexible plan.

## 2. Why a website (¾–1 page)
- The audience is busy parents who search the web at 10 p.m.; a website meets them where they already look, unlike a podcast or poster.
- Interactivity makes the concept personal: the Language Plan Builder turns an abstract framework into *their* beliefs, practices, and management — and ends with a printable artifact they can stick on a fridge.
- Design decisions as teaching decisions: three colored strands as a visual mnemonic for the three components; myth-versus-reality format because the course's own Sept. 1 lecture framed SLA through misconceptions; a Sources section with "how we used it" notes to model the research–practice interface from Module 5.
- Accessibility (keyboard, contrast, mobile) and privacy (nothing stored or sent) as part of treating parents as a real audience, not a pretend one.

## 3. How SLA research was integrated (1½–2 pages)
- Course readings: Ortega (2013) for the global normality of multilingualism, the monolingual bias, and the heritage context; Pavlenko (2015) for how media hype distorts expectations; Gass et al. (2020, Ch. 4) for active grammar construction, the fuzzy simultaneous/sequential boundary, and the "younger is better" nuance; De Houwer (2009) for BFLA milestones, dilingual conversations, discourse strategies, and impact beliefs; Montee's Sept. 1 lecture for vocabulary-per-language versus total vocabulary and the limits of edutainment.
- Independently found articles and what each did on the site: King et al. (2008) as the field-defining framework; De Houwer (2007) to dismantle OPOL-as-rule; Hoff et al. (2012) and Place & Hoff (2011) for input quantity, speaker number, and fluent-speaker share; De Houwer (2015) for harmonious bilingual development and well-being; Byers-Heinlein (2013) for the honest, modest caveat about mixing and the gap between research samples and real homes.
- A paragraph on *restraint*: what you deliberately did not claim (cognitive advantages; unverified percentages; Krashen's affective filter) and why — the research-practice interface is also about not overselling.
- Where the research is mixed and how the site says so (mixing; cognitive effects; age).

## 4. Who the project serves (½–¾ page)
- Primary: current and future parents in multilingual households, especially heritage-language families where the minority language is the one at risk (De Houwer, 2007).
- Secondary: grandparents and caregivers; teachers and pediatric professionals who field these questions; the composite scenarios were written to be inclusive across language pairs, family structures, and fluency levels.
- Reflection on limits: English-language site; assumes internet access; composite scenarios rather than community-specific guidance.

## 5. Use as a teaching tool outside the class (¾ page)
- Parent-education settings: pediatric waiting rooms, heritage schools, community centers, new-parent classes — the Plan Builder as a 10-minute workshop activity, the printable worksheet as the take-home.
- Undergraduate settings: as a model of translating SLA findings for non-experts; the research-notes claim map as an exercise in source transparency.
- Possible extensions: translations of the interface; adding adolescence/heritage-learner content from later course readings (e.g., Anya, 2011); a clinician-facing page.

## 6. Closing (¼–½ page)
- What you learned about the research–practice interface by having to source every sentence.
- Return to the thesis: a plan, not a rule.
